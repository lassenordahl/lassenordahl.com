I went to Config for work this June. As a frontend engineer it was really cool to think more about design within my own work. One of the interesting things about Palantir is we do a little bit of everything, so some of the talks on maps, design system contributions, edge computing, and shaders all felt pretty tangible.

I feel like a lot of my opinions on design and frontend end up being pretty based in viewing human brains as wanting to experience tech a certain way. One of my main feelings after the first few talks was that we really need tangible, physical things to interact with or our brains get unhappy. This is what really led me to liking 3D work, as I can see physical shapes move around.

In my opinion this also plays out with writing code. Generally I think my brain was happier when I moved the code myself, physically, around in an editor. I'm not super convinced that our brains are meant to interact with constantly reading and digesting large blocks of text coming out of Claude/Codex.

Although, I do think that this will go down as tokens get faster. I'm pretty interested to see if intelligence takes a step back compared to token speed improvements for most work use cases. Although I suspect focusing on higher intelligence makes speed easier from the lab POV.
